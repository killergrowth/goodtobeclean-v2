## What Does Air Duct Cleaning Actually Cost in Wichita?

If you've been Googling this question, you've probably already run into a wide range of numbers — and maybe a few suspiciously cheap offers. So let's cut through it.

In Wichita, KS, **professional air duct cleaning typically runs between $300 and $600 for a standard residential home.** Larger homes, commercial properties, or systems with significant buildup can push that number higher. What you pay depends on a few key factors — and knowing them ahead of time helps you spot a fair quote versus a bait-and-switch.

---

## What Affects the Price?

### Home Size & Number of Vents
Most companies price by the vent or by square footage. A 1,500 sq ft home with 10–12 vents is going to cost less than a 3,000 sq ft home with 20+ supply and return registers. Expect roughly **$25–$45 per vent** from a reputable provider.

### Type of Ductwork
Flex duct, sheet metal, and older fiberglass duct board each require slightly different approaches. Older or more fragile systems take more care and time, which can affect the final price.

### Level of Contamination
If your ducts haven't been cleaned in 10+ years — or you've recently dealt with water damage, mold, or a rodent issue — the job is more involved. Heavy debris, microbial growth, or post-construction dust all require additional steps and equipment.

### Add-On Services
Some companies offer optional add-ons like:
- **Sanitizing/deodorizing treatments** ($50–$150)
- **Dryer vent cleaning** ($80–$150)
- **Coil cleaning** (evaporator/condenser)

These can be worth it depending on your situation — just make sure they're itemized clearly before you agree.

---

## The $49 Special: Why to Be Skeptical

You've seen the ads. "$49 whole-house duct cleaning!" It sounds great. It almost never is.

These low-ball offers are a common upsell trap. A crew shows up, does a surface-level cleaning with basic equipment, and then tells you that you actually need $400–$800 in add-ons to "really" clean your system. Legitimate air duct cleaning requires truck-mounted or commercial-grade negative pressure equipment — that equipment doesn't come cheap, and neither does the trained labor to run it properly.

A fair price from a reputable Wichita company reflects real work. If a quote seems too low to be real, trust that instinct.

---

## When Does Air Duct Cleaning Actually Make Sense?

Not every home needs it every year. The EPA and NADCA generally recommend cleaning when you notice:

- Visible dust or debris blowing from registers
- Musty or stale odors when the HVAC runs
- Recent water intrusion, mold, or flooding (in that case, [water damage restoration](/service/water-damage-restoration/) may also be needed before cleaning)
- A new home purchase or recent renovation
- Household members with allergies, asthma, or respiratory issues
- It's been 3–5+ years since the last cleaning

If you've had moisture issues in your home, it's also worth having someone check for mold in the ductwork before doing a standard cleaning — circulating air through contaminated ducts spreads the problem. ## What's Included in a Professional Cleaning?

A thorough [air duct cleaning](/service/air-duct-cleaning/) should include:

- Inspection of the duct system before and after
- Negative pressure (vacuum) applied to the main trunk line
- Cleaning of all supply and return registers
- Brushing or agitation of duct walls to dislodge debris
- Cleaning of the air handler, blower, and accessible coils (varies by provider)
- Post-cleaning walkthrough

Ask any company you're considering what's specifically included — a good one won't hesitate to tell you.
