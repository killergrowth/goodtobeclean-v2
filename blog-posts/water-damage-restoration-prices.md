If water just flooded your basement or a pipe burst behind your wall, the last thing you want is a vague answer. ## The Biggest Factors That Affect Your Price

No two water damage jobs are identical. Here's what restoration companies — including us here in El Dorado and Wichita — look at when building an estimate:

### 1. Category of Water
Water damage is classified into three categories, and pricing follows them closely:

- **Category 1 (Clean Water)** — Burst supply lines, overflowing sinks. Easiest and least expensive to remediate. Avg: $1,000–$2,500.
- **Category 2 (Gray Water)** — Washing machine overflow, dishwasher leaks, mild flooding. More contamination involved. Avg: $2,000–$4,500.
- **Category 3 (Black Water)** — Sewage backups, floodwater from outside, standing water that's been sitting. Requires full protective protocols. Avg: $3,500–$7,500+.

### 2. Square Footage Affected
Most restoration companies price per square foot for drying and mitigation. Expect **$3.75–$7.50/sq ft** for water extraction and structural drying. A 500 sq ft basement flooded with clean water is a very different job than 1,500 sq ft of finished living space with saturated drywall and subfloor.

### 3. How Long the Water Sat
Time is everything. Water that's been sitting for 24–48 hours has already begun damaging drywall, subfloor, and framing — and may have started growing mold. Delayed response dramatically increases scope (and cost). If mold has taken hold, you're looking at a separate [mold removal](/service/mold-removal/) process on top of the restoration work.

### 4. What Needs to Be Replaced vs. Dried
Mitigation (drying, extracting, dehumidifying) is one cost bucket. Reconstruction — replacing flooring, drywall, insulation, cabinetry — is another. Many companies separate these into two distinct phases. Make sure your quote specifies both.

## Does Insurance Cover It?

Usually — with conditions. Homeowners insurance typically covers **sudden and accidental** water damage (burst pipe, appliance failure, storm damage that breaches the structure). It does **not** cover gradual leaks, flooding from outside, or damage caused by neglect.

A few things to do right away:
- Document everything with photos and video before any cleanup begins
- Call your insurance company to open a claim
- Get a written estimate from your restoration company

A reputable [water damage restoration](/service/water-damage-restoration/) company will work directly with your adjuster and help document the loss properly — that matters more than most people realize.
