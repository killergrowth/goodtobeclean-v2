# What's Included in Residential Water Damage Restoration Services?

When water invades your home, understanding what professional **residential water damage restoration services** actually cover makes the difference between a complete recovery and lingering problems. Whether you're dealing with a burst pipe, storm flooding, or a failed appliance, restoration companies offer distinct service levels that homeowners in Wichita and south-central Kansas need to understand before making the call. As an IICRC-certified restoration company, Good To Be Clean follows industry-standard protocols that separate emergency response from full restoration work. This guide breaks down exactly what's included in professional water damage services, what falls outside typical scope, and how to navigate the process from your first call to final sign-off.

## What Services Are Included in Water Damage Restoration?

Professional [water damage restoration](/water-damage-restoration/) encompasses a multi-phase process that addresses both immediate damage and long-term structural concerns. The scope typically includes water extraction using industrial pumps and vacuums, structural drying with commercial-grade dehumidifiers and air movers, moisture monitoring with thermal imaging and hygrometers, antimicrobial treatment to prevent mold growth, and content manipulation (moving furniture and belongings to prevent further damage).

The process begins with a thorough assessment that identifies the water categoryclean water from supply lines, gray water from appliances, or black water from sewage or floodingsince this determines the necessary safety protocols. Technicians document moisture levels in walls, floors, and ceilings, tracking these readings daily until materials return to acceptable dry standards. IICRC-certified teams follow specific drying goals based on material type: hardwood floors require different treatment than drywall or carpet.

**Home water damage restoration services** also include detailed documentation for insurance purposes. Restoration companies photograph damage, log equipment placement and runtime, track daily moisture readings, and provide written scope reports. This documentation proves critical when filing claims, as insurers require evidence that mitigation happened promptly and followed industry standards.

## Emergency Water Damage Services vs. Standard Restoration Work

Emergency water damage restoration differs fundamentally from scheduled restoration projects. Emergency services operate 24/7 and focus exclusively on damage mitigationstopping the source, extracting standing water, and beginning the drying process within hours of your call. Speed matters because water wicks through porous materials continuously. Drywall absorbs moisture vertically at roughly one inch per hour, meaning a delay of even 12 hours can turn a partial wall replacement into a full-room gut.

Standard restoration work happens after initial mitigation stabilizes the structure. This phase includes reconstruction services like drywall replacement, flooring installation, painting, and trim work. However, not all restoration companies perform reconstruction. Some focus strictly on **water damage mitigation services**the extraction, drying, and antimicrobial treatmentthen refer reconstruction to contractors. Good To Be Clean handles both mitigation and reconstruction, streamlining the timeline and simplifying communication for homeowners managing insurance claims.

The distinction matters when choosing a provider. If you need both services from one team, verify capabilities upfront. Ask whether they employ licensed contractors or subcontract reconstruction work. Companies that self-perform both phases typically complete projects faster since they don't wait for third-party scheduling.

## What Water Damage Companies Don't Typically Cover

Understanding scope limitations prevents surprise costs. Residential water damage restoration services generally don't include plumbing repairs to fix the source of the leakyou'll need a licensed plumber for burst pipes or fixture failures. Similarly, roof repairs for leak sources require a roofing contractor, and HVAC issues causing condensation problems need HVAC specialists.

Restoration teams also don't handle insurance deductibles or guarantee full coverage. They work with adjusters and provide documentation, but the final claim decision rests with your insurance company. Some cosmetic improvements fall outside standard scope too. If you want to upgrade flooring materials or change paint colors during restoration, those enhancements typically require separate agreements and additional costs beyond what insurance covers.

Content cleaning and storage services vary by company. Some restoration providers offer pack-out services, removing and storing belongings during the drying process, while others focus exclusively on structural work. For [water damage restoration in Wichita](/water-damage-restoration/wichita-ks/), verify these details when requesting estimates, especially if you need temporary storage or specialized content cleaning for items like electronics or documents.

## How the Process Works From First Call to Final Sign-Off

When you contact a restoration company, expect an immediate response for emergency situations. The first technician visit includes source identification, safety assessment, and initial moisture mapping. They'll place extraction and drying equipment, typically within 24 hours for emergency calls. The mitigation phase runs 37 days on average, though timeline varies based on affected materials and moisture severity.

Daily monitoring visits track drying progress. Technicians adjust equipment, take fresh moisture readings, and document conditions. Once materials reach dry standardstypically below 15% moisture content for wood and 1% for concretethe team removes equipment and provides a completion certificate. This certificate proves essential for insurance claims, documenting that mitigation met industry standards and prevents future mold growth liability.

Reconstruction scheduling depends on insurance approval and material availability. Straightforward jobs like drywall replacement and painting might take 25 days. More complex projects involving flooring, cabinets, or custom millwork extend timelines. Throughout the process, IICRC-certified teams maintain communication with both homeowners and adjusters, updating everyone on progress and any scope changes discovered during demolition.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What services are included in water damage restoration?

Water damage restoration includes water extraction, structural drying with commercial dehumidifiers and air movers, moisture monitoring using specialized meters, antimicrobial treatment, content manipulation, and thorough documentation for insurance claims. IICRC-certified companies follow industry protocols to dry structures to acceptable moisture levels, typically completing the process in 37 days depending on severity and materials affected.

### Do water damage companies do repairs too?

Some water damage companies perform both mitigation and reconstruction, while others focus exclusively on drying and refer repair work to contractors. Mitigation includes extraction and drying, while reconstruction covers drywall replacement, flooring installation, painting, and trim work. Companies that self-perform both phases typically complete projects faster by eliminating third-party scheduling delays.

### What is emergency water damage service?

Emergency water damage service provides 24/7 response focused on immediate mitigationstopping the water source, extracting standing water, and starting the drying process within hours. Speed is critical because water continues spreading through porous materials, with drywall absorbing moisture at roughly one inch per hour. Emergency services prioritize damage containment before transitioning to standard restoration phases.

### How do I file an insurance claim for water damage?

Contact your insurance company immediately to report the loss and receive a claim number. Document damage with photos before restoration begins. Your restoration company will provide detailed scope reports, moisture readings, and equipment logs that support your claim. The adjuster reviews this documentation to determine coverage, though the restoration team can answer questions and coordinate directly with adjusters throughout the process.

### What is included in water damage mitigation vs restoration?

Water damage mitigation focuses on stopping damage progression through extraction, drying, and antimicrobial treatment. Restoration includes the rebuilding phasereplacing damaged materials, painting, and returning the structure to pre-loss condition. Mitigation happens immediately and typically takes 37 days, while restoration begins after materials reach dry standards and may take additional weeks depending on reconstruction scope.
