## So You Found Black Mold. Now What?

It usually starts with a smell. That musty, earthy odor creeping out of the bathroom, basement, or under the kitchen sink. Then you look closer and there it is — dark patches on the wall, ceiling, or grout that weren't there before.

Your first instinct might be to grab some bleach and handle it yourself. And honestly? That instinct makes sense. It's your home, the stuff is right there, and a quick Google search makes it look easy enough.

But before you suit up and start scrubbing, let's talk through what black mold removal DIY actually involves — and where it goes wrong for a lot of Kansas homeowners.

---

## What "Black Mold" Really Means

First, a clarification: not every dark mold is *Stachybotrys chartarum*, the notorious "toxic black mold" you've probably heard about. Mold comes in hundreds of species and many are dark-colored. You can't tell by looking at it.

What you *can* tell is whether it's spreading, whether there's moisture fueling it, and whether it's in a contained area or has gotten into your walls, insulation, or HVAC system. That last part is where DIY black mold removal in Wichita, KS starts to get complicated.

---

## When DIY Mold Removal *Might* Be Reasonable

To be fair — not every mold situation requires a professional. The EPA generally says mold covering less than 10 square feet (roughly a 3x3 patch) in a non-porous, accessible area *can* be handled by a careful homeowner.

That looks like:
- Surface mold on a tile backsplash or caulk line
- A small patch on a painted wall with no sign of spreading behind it
- Mold on a hard, non-porous bathroom surface

If that's your situation, here's what proper DIY looks like:

1. **Gear up first** — N95 respirator (not a dust mask), nitrile gloves, and safety glasses. Mold spores go airborne when disturbed.
2. **Don't use straight bleach** — Bleach kills surface mold but doesn't penetrate porous materials. Use an EPA-registered mold cleaner or a diluted hydrogen peroxide solution.
3. **Fix the moisture source** — If you don't fix what caused it, the mold will come back within weeks. Guaranteed.
4. **Bag and seal everything** — Used materials go into heavy-duty plastic bags before they leave the room.
5. **Dry thoroughly** — Mold needs moisture. After cleaning, dehumidify the area completely.

---

## Where DIY Goes Wrong

Here's the honest part. Most homeowners who call us after a DIY attempt made one of a few common mistakes:

**They didn't contain the area.** Scrubbing mold without sealing off the room sends spores through your HVAC and into every room in the house.

**They cleaned the surface but not the source.** Mold growing on drywall usually means mold *inside* the drywall. Wiping the face doesn't fix it.

**They didn't find all of it.** Mold often grows behind walls, under flooring, and in crawl spaces. What you see is often just a fraction of what's actually there.

**They underestimated the size.** A small visible patch near a leaking pipe can mean extensive growth behind the wall. Without pulling materials, you won't know.

This is especially true in older homes around the Wichita area where pipe insulation, older drywall, and inconsistent ventilation create ideal conditions for hidden mold growth.
