## When Water Damage Hits, the Clock Starts Ticking

A burst pipe. A backed-up sewer line. A storm that pushed water through your foundation. Whatever caused it, water damage in your Wichita home is one of those situations where every hour counts — and so does every decision you make in the first 24 hours.

One of the biggest mistakes homeowners make? Waiting too long to call their insurance company, or skipping critical documentation steps before cleanup begins. This guide walks you through exactly how to file a water damage insurance claim the right way so you're not leaving money on the table.

---

## Step 1: Stop the Source (If It's Safe)

Before you document or call anyone, stop the water if you can. Shut off the main water valve, turn off power to affected areas if there's standing water near electrical outlets, and move valuables out of harm's way. If the source is a roof leak, active flooding, or a sewage backup, don't risk your safety — get out and call for help.

---

## Step 2: Document Everything Before Cleanup Begins

This step is non-negotiable. Insurance adjusters work off evidence, and once cleanup starts, that evidence disappears.

- **Take photos and video of all affected areas** — floors, walls, ceilings, furniture, and personal items
- **Note the date and approximate time** the damage occurred or was discovered
- **Don't throw anything away** until your adjuster has seen it or you've documented it thoroughly
- **Write down what happened** in your own words — even a quick voice memo helps

The more documentation you have, the stronger your claim.

---

## Step 3: Call Your Insurance Company ASAP

Most homeowner policies have reporting deadlines. Call your insurance provider as soon as possible — ideally within 24 hours of discovery. When you call:

- Have your policy number ready
- Describe what happened and when
- Ask specifically whether your policy covers the type of water damage you experienced (sudden/accidental vs. flooding vs. sewage backup — these are often covered differently)
- Get a claim number and the adjuster's contact information

**Wichita-specific note:** Standard homeowner insurance typically does NOT cover flooding from rising rivers or stormwater. If your home flooded due to weather, you may need a separate NFIP (National Flood Insurance Program) policy. Check your coverage before assuming you're protected.

---

## Step 4: Mitigate Further Damage — and Keep Receipts

Your insurance policy likely requires you to take "reasonable steps" to prevent additional damage. That means you can — and should — call a professional [water damage restoration](/service/water-damage-restoration/) company to begin extraction and drying even before your adjuster arrives.

Keep receipts for everything: temporary repairs, hotel stays, fans or dehumidifiers you rent, and any emergency services. These costs are typically reimbursable under "additional living expenses" or "mitigation costs" in your policy.

---

## Step 5: Meet With the Insurance Adjuster

Your insurance company will send an adjuster to assess the damage. A few tips for this visit:

- **Walk them through everything** — don't let them rush
- **Point out hidden damage** like wet insulation inside walls, subfloor saturation, or HVAC contamination
- **Have your documentation ready** to share
- **Don't feel pressured to accept a settlement on the spot** — you can negotiate or request a re-inspection

If you feel the estimate is low, you're allowed to bring in a public adjuster or get your own repair estimate from a licensed contractor.

## Common Reasons Claims Get Denied (And How to Avoid Them)

- **Delayed reporting** — call within 24-48 hours of discovery
- **Pre-existing damage** — document that the damage is new and sudden
- **Lack of maintenance** — slow leaks over time are often excluded; sudden pipe bursts usually aren't
- **Wrong coverage type** — confirm whether you have flood insurance vs. standard homeowner coverage
