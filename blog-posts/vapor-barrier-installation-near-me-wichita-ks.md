## What Is a Vapor Barrier and What Does It Actually Do?

A vapor barrier — sometimes called a crawl space liner — is a heavy-duty polyethylene sheet installed across the floor and walls of your crawl space. Its job is simple: block ground moisture from evaporating up into your home's framing, insulation, and subfloor.

Without it, that moisture has nowhere to go. It condenses, it lingers, and over time it creates the exact conditions that mold and rot need to thrive.

**Signs you may need a vapor barrier:**
- Musty odors coming from your floors or vents
- Visible moisture, condensation, or standing water in your crawl space
- Higher-than-normal energy bills (moist insulation loses effectiveness fast)
- Wood rot or soft spots in your flooring
- Allergy symptoms that worsen indoors

In the Wichita area, where soil moisture fluctuates dramatically through the seasons, a properly installed vapor barrier is less of an upgrade and more of a necessity.

## What to Look for When Hiring a Vapor Barrier Installer in Wichita

This is where homeowners often get tripped up. Vapor barrier installation looks straightforward, but a bad install can leave gaps, trap existing moisture, or miss underlying issues that will come back to bite you. Here's what to ask before you hire:

### 1. Do They Inspect Before They Install?

A reputable company won't just lay plastic and call it done. They should assess your crawl space for existing moisture damage, mold, standing water, and drainage issues first. Installing a vapor barrier over an active moisture problem is like putting a bandage over an infection.

### 2. What Mil Thickness Do They Use?

Vapor barriers are rated by thickness in mils. Most professionals recommend a minimum of 6-mil for basic protection, but 10-20 mil is the standard for long-term durability, especially in climate-variable regions like south-central Kansas. Thicker material resists punctures and lasts significantly longer.

### 3. Will They Seal the Walls and Seams?

The barrier should extend up the crawl space walls and be sealed at every seam with tape or adhesive. A barrier that only covers the floor leaves plenty of entry points for moisture to sneak in through the foundation walls.

### 4. Do They Handle Related Issues?

If there's already mold present, you'll want a company that can handle [mold removal](/service/mold-removal/) as part of the same job — or at least before the barrier goes in. Companies that only do one piece of the puzzle can leave you coordinating multiple contractors and potentially missing something important.
