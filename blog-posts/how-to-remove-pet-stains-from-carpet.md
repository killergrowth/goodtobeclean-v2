## Pet Accidents Are Part of Life — Here's How to Handle Them

If you're a pet owner in Wichita, KS, you already know the drill. You come home, you spot the wet patch on the carpet, and the clock is already ticking. Whether it's a puppy that didn't quite make it outside or an older dog with a surprise accident, how fast you act — and what you use — makes all the difference between a clean carpet and a permanent stain.

Here's a straightforward guide on how to remove pet stains from carpet, written for real homeowners dealing with real messes.

---

## Step 1: Act Fast and Blot (Don't Rub)

The moment you find a fresh accident, grab a clean white cloth or paper towels and start blotting. Press firmly and work from the outside of the stain inward — this keeps it from spreading.

**Do not scrub.** Scrubbing pushes the urine deeper into the carpet fibers and can damage the pile. Blot, lift, repeat until you've pulled up as much liquid as possible.

---

## Step 2: Rinse With Cold Water

Once you've blotted up the bulk of it, pour a small amount of cold water over the area to dilute what's left. Blot again. Don't soak the carpet — you're rinsing, not flooding.

Avoid hot water at this stage. Heat can set protein-based stains and lock odors into the fibers.

---

## Step 3: Apply an Enzyme-Based Cleaner

This is the most important step most people skip. Enzyme cleaners break down the uric acid crystals in pet urine — the same crystals that cause that lingering smell even after the visible stain is gone.

You can find enzyme cleaners at most pet stores or online. Apply generously, let it sit for 10–15 minutes (read the label), then blot clean.

**Avoid:** vinegar and baking soda alone won't neutralize uric acid. They can mask the smell temporarily but won't eliminate the source.

---

## Step 4: Dry Thoroughly

After cleaning, place a stack of paper towels over the area, weigh them down with something heavy (like a book), and let them sit for several hours. You want the area as dry as possible to prevent mold or mildew growth underneath.

A fan or open window speeds things up. If you have a wet/dry vac, use it.

---

## Step 5: Check With a Black Light

Once dry, use a UV black light in a dark room to check for any residual urine you might have missed. Pet urine glows under black light — this is how professionals identify all the spots that need treatment.

If you find additional spots, repeat the enzyme cleaner process on each one.

---

## When DIY Isn't Enough

Sometimes the stain is old, the pet has used the same spot repeatedly, or the urine has soaked through to the pad or subfloor. At that point, surface cleaning won't cut it. The odor comes back every time the humidity rises, no matter how much enzyme cleaner you apply.
