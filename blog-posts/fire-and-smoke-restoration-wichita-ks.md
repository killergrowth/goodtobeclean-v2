## The Moment After a Fire Is Overwhelming — Here's What Comes Next

A fire doesn't have to be catastrophic to cause serious damage. Even a contained kitchen fire or an electrical incident in a single room can leave smoke, soot, and odor throughout your entire home. For homeowners in Wichita and the surrounding area, knowing what to expect from the [fire and smoke restoration](/service/fire-and-smoke-restoration/) process can make an already difficult situation feel a lot more manageable.

This guide walks you through every phase — from the moment the fire is out to the day you're back to normal.

---

## Step 1: Emergency Contact and Initial Assessment

The first 24–48 hours after a fire are critical. The longer soot and smoke residue sit on surfaces, the deeper they penetrate — and the harder they are to remove.

When you call Good To Be Clean, we respond quickly and get eyes on the damage right away. Our team conducts a full assessment of:

- Structural damage to walls, ceilings, and flooring
- Smoke and soot distribution throughout the home
- Water damage from firefighting efforts
- Air quality and odor severity

This assessment drives the entire restoration plan. No two fires are the same, and we don't treat them that way.

---

## Step 2: Securing and Stabilizing the Property

Before any cleaning begins, the property has to be safe to work in. That may mean boarding up windows, tarping the roof, or isolating sections of the home to prevent further contamination.

If firefighting water caused secondary flooding, [water damage restoration](/service/water-damage-restoration/) work often runs parallel to the fire cleanup. Standing water left untreated becomes a mold problem within 24–48 hours — so we address both simultaneously when needed.

---

## Step 3: Soot and Smoke Removal

This is where the real work happens — and where cutting corners creates long-term problems.

Soot is acidic. Left on walls, countertops, and furniture, it continues to cause damage and discoloration even after the fire is out. Smoke odor penetrates porous materials like drywall, insulation, upholstery, and wood framing.

Our process includes:

- **Dry cleaning and wet cleaning** of all affected surfaces
- **HEPA vacuuming** to capture fine soot particles
- **Thermal fogging and ozone treatment** for deep odor neutralization
- **Air scrubbing** to improve indoor air quality throughout the structure

This isn't a wipe-down job. Proper smoke removal requires professional-grade equipment and trained technicians who know how different materials respond to different cleaning methods.

---

## Step 4: Content Cleaning and Pack-Out (When Needed)

Furniture, clothing, documents, electronics — personal belongings caught in a fire often need specialized attention. In some cases, we remove contents from the home entirely for off-site cleaning and storage while the structural restoration is completed.

Items that look unsalvageable are often recoverable with the right process. We inventory everything carefully and work to restore as much as possible.

---

## Step 5: Structural Repairs and Reconstruction

Once the home is cleaned, deodorized, and dried, any damaged structural elements are repaired. This can range from drywall patching and repainting to replacing flooring, cabinetry, or fixtures.

The goal is simple: return your home to the condition it was in before the fire — or better.

## How Long Does Fire Restoration Take?

It depends on the size and severity of the fire, but here's a general range:

- **Minor smoke damage (1–2 rooms):** 3–7 days
- **Moderate fire damage (partial structure):** 2–4 weeks
- **Severe or total loss scenarios:** 4–12+ weeks
