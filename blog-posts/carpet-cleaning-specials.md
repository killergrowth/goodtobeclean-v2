## What the "Special" Actually Costs You

You've seen the ads — "$49 for three rooms!" or a Groupon coupon promising a whole-house clean for the price of lunch. It sounds like a deal. And sometimes it is. But more often than not, those teaser prices come with strings attached that don't show up until the technician is already in your home.

Here's how it usually goes: the "special" covers a base treatment only. The moment they spot a stain, heavy traffic areas, pet odors, or anything beyond bone-dry carpet sitting in a museum, the add-ons start stacking up. By the time the job is done, you're paying two or three times what the ad promised — and you felt pressured to say yes because someone's already on your floor with equipment.

That's not a deal. That's bait-and-switch.

This guide will walk you through what a legitimate carpet cleaning special looks like, what red flags to watch for, and what actually matters when you're comparing options in the Wichita area.

---

## What to Look for in a Carpet Cleaning Deal

**Transparent, flat-rate pricing.** A reputable company will quote you based on square footage or room count — and that number shouldn't change dramatically once they arrive. Ask upfront what's included: pre-treatment, hot water extraction, deodorizing, and drying time. If they can't tell you clearly, that's a red flag.

**Hot water extraction (steam cleaning).** This is the gold standard for deep cleaning. It removes dirt, allergens, and bacteria from the carpet fibers themselves — not just the surface. Some discount services use dry-foam or bonnet cleaning, which looks good temporarily but doesn't actually clean deep. Know what method you're getting.

**Realistic expectations on drying time.** Professional carpet cleaning leaves carpets slightly damp for a few hours. If someone promises "dry in 20 minutes," they're either not cleaning deeply or they're using a method that won't last. Honest companies will tell you to plan for 4–6 hours of drying time.

**Reviews that back it up.** Anyone can print a coupon. Look at the reviews — not just the star rating, but what people are actually saying. Are customers mentioning honest pricing? Did the crew do what they said they'd do? Did anything get added on unexpectedly?

---

## What to Avoid

**Groupon and coupon-first companies.** We're not saying every discount offer is a scam, but companies that rely heavily on Groupon deals are often working on razor-thin margins. That creates pressure to upsell aggressively once they're in the door. If the business model depends on getting you to say yes to extras, your interests aren't aligned.

**Franchise pricing games.** Some national franchise brands advertise low prices but calculate costs by the square foot with complicated zone-based systems. What looks like a $99 special can balloon fast in an average-sized home. Always ask for a full estimate in writing before anyone shows up.

**Vague "per room" pricing without size limits.** "Per room" pricing is only meaningful if you know how they define a room. Some companies cap rooms at 150–200 sq ft — meaning your master bedroom or open-concept living area counts as two or three rooms. Read the fine print or ask directly.
