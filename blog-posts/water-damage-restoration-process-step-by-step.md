## When Water Damage Hits, Knowing What Comes Next Helps

A pipe bursts. A basement floods. Water creeps under doors after a heavy Kansas storm. Whatever triggered it, the first few hours after water damage are disorienting — and most homeowners have no idea what the restoration process actually looks like.

That uncertainty makes a hard situation harder.

This guide walks you through the [water damage restoration](/service/water-damage-restoration/) process step by step — exactly what happens from the moment a crew arrives at your Wichita home to the day everything is back to normal.

---

## Step 1: Emergency Contact & Rapid Response

The process starts the second you call. A reputable restoration company will ask you a few quick questions — where the water is coming from, how much area is affected, whether the source has been stopped — and dispatch a crew immediately.

For Wichita homeowners, fast response matters. Kansas humidity accelerates mold growth, and water that sits even 24–48 hours can cause significantly more structural damage. This is why 24/7 availability isn't just a marketing line — it's the difference between a manageable repair and a major gut job.

---

## Step 2: Damage Assessment & Inspection

When the crew arrives, the first thing they do is assess — not start pulling things apart.

Technicians use moisture meters, thermal imaging cameras, and visual inspection to map the full extent of the damage. Water travels in ways you can't see. It wicks into drywall, soaks into subfloor, and hides behind cabinets. A thorough assessment ensures nothing gets missed.

This step also determines the **water damage category**:
- **Category 1** – Clean water (burst supply line, overflowed sink)
- **Category 2** – Gray water (appliance discharge, minor flooding)
- **Category 3** – Black water (sewage, floodwater) — requires more intensive remediation protocols

---

## Step 3: Water Extraction

Once the scope is mapped, extraction begins. Industrial-grade truck-mounted or portable extraction units remove standing water fast — far more effectively than any shop vac or consumer pump.

This step is the most time-sensitive. The faster water is removed, the less opportunity it has to saturate structural materials and create conditions for mold.

In severe cases, crews may also remove saturated carpet padding and sections of drywall during this phase if they're beyond saving.

---

## Step 4: Drying & Dehumidification

Extraction removes bulk water. Drying removes what's left behind.

High-capacity air movers and commercial dehumidifiers are placed strategically throughout the affected area. This phase typically runs **3–5 days**, depending on the extent of saturation, the materials involved, and ambient conditions.

Technicians return daily to check moisture readings and adjust equipment placement. Skipping this step — or cutting it short — is one of the most common reasons mold problems show up weeks later.

---

## Step 5: Mold Prevention & Antimicrobial Treatment

Even with proper drying, restoration professionals treat affected areas with antimicrobial and antifungal agents as a precaution. In Wichita's climate, this step isn't optional — it's standard practice.

If mold is already present, this phase expands into full [mold removal](/service/mold-removal/), which involves containment, HEPA filtration, and controlled removal of affected materials. Catching it early during the drying phase is always preferable.

---

## Step 6: Cleaning & Sanitizing

Water damage — especially from Category 2 or 3 sources — leaves behind contaminants, odors, and residue. Technicians clean and sanitize all salvageable surfaces, belongings, and structural elements before any rebuilding begins.

This often includes deodorization treatments to eliminate the musty smell that lingers long after water is gone.

---

## Step 7: Restoration & Repairs

The final phase is getting your home back to pre-loss condition. Depending on the severity, this could mean:

- Replacing drywall and insulation
- Installing new flooring or carpet
- Repainting walls
- Repairing or replacing cabinetry and trim
- Structural repairs in severe cases

Companies that handle both mitigation and [full restoration services](/service/restoration-services/) can take you through the entire process under one roof — no handing off between contractors mid-project.
