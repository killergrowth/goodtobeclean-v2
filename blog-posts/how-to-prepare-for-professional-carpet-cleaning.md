## A Little Prep Goes a Long Way

You've booked the appointment. Professional carpet cleaning day is coming. But here's what a lot of homeowners don't realize — what you do *before* the technician walks through the door can make a noticeable difference in your results.

Whether you're getting your carpets cleaned for the first time or you've done it before, this guide walks you through exactly how to prepare for professional carpet cleaning so you get the most out of every square foot.

---

## 1. Clear the Furniture (as Much as You Can)

The more floor space the technician can access, the better the clean. Before your appointment:

- Move lightweight furniture like chairs, end tables, ottomans, and floor lamps out of the room
- Leave heavy pieces like sofas and beds in place — most professionals have sliders and will work around them
- Remove breakables from nearby shelves or tables, just in case

You don't have to empty the entire room. Just clear the obvious stuff and let the pros handle the rest.

---

## 2. Vacuum First

Yes — even though you're about to get a deep clean, vacuuming ahead of time matters.

Professional carpet cleaning (especially hot water extraction / steam cleaning) works best when loose surface debris is already gone. Vacuuming first lets the machine focus on deep-down dirt, allergens, and grime rather than surface-level dust and pet hair.

Give every room a solid pass, and don't skip the edges and corners.

---

## 3. Point Out Problem Areas

Take a few minutes to walk through your home and make a mental note — or actual sticky notes — of any spots that need extra attention:

- Pet stains or odors
- Old set-in stains (coffee, wine, mud)
- High-traffic zones with visible wear or discoloration
- Any areas you've already tried to treat yourself

When your technician arrives, walk them through these spots. A good cleaning crew wants to know. Communicating upfront helps them choose the right pre-treatment and technique for each area.

---

## 4. Secure Pets and Kids

It sounds simple, but it's worth saying: carpet cleaning equipment is loud, and the process involves hoses, hot water, and chemicals that shouldn't be touched mid-process.

Plan ahead:
- Arrange for pets to stay in a gated area, garage, or with a neighbor
- Keep young kids out of the rooms being cleaned until they're fully dry
- Drying time typically runs 4–8 hours depending on airflow and humidity — Kansas summers can actually speed this up if you can crack a window

---

## 5. Pre-Treat Stains If You Want To (But Don't Overdo It)

If you've got a specific stain you've been fighting, a light pre-treatment from a store-bought solution is fine — but don't go overboard. Over-saturating a stain with DIY products before a professional clean can actually make it harder to remove and may interact with professional-grade chemistry.

When in doubt, leave it alone and let the pros handle it. That's what they're there for.

---

## 6. Make Parking and Access Easy

This one gets overlooked. Professional carpet cleaning rigs — especially truck-mounted systems — need to be parked close to your front door. The hose typically runs from the truck into your home.

- Clear your driveway if possible
- Let neighbors know if parking on the street may be tight
- Make sure your entryway is easy to access and clear of tripping hazards

---

## 7. Plan for Dry Time

Your carpets won't be immediately ready to walk on after cleaning. Give yourself and your family a buffer:

- Keep windows open and fans running to speed drying
- Walk on carpets in socks if you need to pass through
- Avoid dragging furniture back until carpets are fully dry to prevent denting and rust marks from metal feet
