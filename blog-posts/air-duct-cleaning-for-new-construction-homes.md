You just moved into a brand-new home. The paint is fresh, the floors are spotless, and everything smells like possibility. So when someone suggests getting your air ducts cleaned before you're even settled in, it's natural to wonder — *do I really need that?*

If your new build is in the Wichita area, the short answer is: probably yes. And the reasons are more straightforward than you might think.

## What Happens Inside Ductwork During Construction

Building a home is messy work. Crews are cutting drywall, sanding surfaces, sawing lumber, and laying insulation — often while the HVAC system is already installed and sitting open. Duct openings are left exposed for weeks or months, and construction dust, drywall particles, sawdust, and insulation fibers settle right into them.

Contractors aren't trying to be careless — it's just the nature of the job. But by the time the house is finished, those ducts can contain a surprising amount of debris that nobody ever cleaned out.

### What's Typically Found in New Construction Ducts

- Fine drywall dust (which circulates easily and coats everything)
- Wood shavings and sawdust
- Fiberglass insulation fragments
- Joint compound residue
- Stray hardware like screws or staples
- Dirt and tracked-in debris from an open construction site

None of that belongs in the air you're breathing — especially if you have kids, pets, or anyone in the house with allergies or asthma.

## Why "New" Doesn't Mean "Clean"

There's a common assumption that new construction equals clean. And structurally, sure — the home is new. But the HVAC system has been sitting in a dusty worksite for months.

When you turn on the heat or AC for the first time, your system pulls air through those ducts and distributes whatever's in them throughout every room in the house. That includes the bedroom where your kids sleep, the kitchen where you cook, and the living room where you spend most of your time.

In many cases, homeowners don't realize the issue until they notice:

- A dusty film settling on surfaces unusually fast
- Musty or stale odors from the vents
- Increased allergy symptoms after moving in
- Reduced airflow from certain registers

By then, they've already been breathing that air for weeks.

## Does Your Builder Clean the Ducts Before Handover?

Some do. Many don't. There's no universal standard requiring builders to clean ductwork before a home is sold, and even when they do wipe things down, it's rarely a thorough cleaning.

If you're buying a new construction home in Wichita — whether in a new development near Andover, a build in Derby, or anywhere in the metro — it's worth asking your builder directly. If they can't confirm the ducts were professionally cleaned, treat them as if they weren't.

## When Should You Schedule Air Duct Cleaning?

Ideally, before you move in — or within the first few weeks of occupancy. This gives you a clean baseline before furniture, rugs, and daily life add their own layer of dust and allergens to the mix.

If you've already been in your new home for a few months and skipped this step, it's not too late. The debris doesn't disappear on its own. A professional cleaning now still removes what's built up and gives your system a fresh start.

## The Bigger Picture: Protecting Your HVAC Investment

A new HVAC system is one of the most expensive components in your home. Construction debris sitting in the ducts doesn't just affect your air quality — it works its way into the system itself, coating coils and clogging filters faster than normal. That means more frequent filter changes, potential efficiency loss, and added wear on equipment that should last 15–20 years.

Cleaning the ducts when the system is new is one of the simplest ways to protect that investment long-term.
