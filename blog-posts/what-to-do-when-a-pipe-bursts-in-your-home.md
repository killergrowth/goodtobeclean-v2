## A Burst Pipe Doesn't Wait — Neither Should You

It can happen on a frigid January night or after years of pressure buildup in aging pipes. One moment everything is fine; the next, water is pouring into your walls, floors, or crawl space. A burst pipe is one of the most common — and most damaging — emergencies a Wichita homeowner can face.

The good news? What you do in the first 30 minutes matters more than almost anything else. This guide walks you through exactly what to do, step by step, so you can stop the damage and protect your home.

---

## Step 1: Shut Off the Water Immediately

Your first move is to stop the source. Head straight to your main water shutoff valve and turn it off. In most Wichita homes, this is located near the water meter — often in the basement, utility room, or near the front of the house by the foundation.

If you don't know where yours is, find it *before* an emergency happens. Post the location somewhere visible or share it with your family now.

Once the main valve is off, open a faucet on the lowest level of your home to drain any remaining water pressure in the lines.

---

## Step 2: Kill the Electricity in Affected Areas

Water and electricity are a dangerous combination. If the burst pipe is near outlets, a breaker panel, or any electrical fixtures, switch off the circuit breakers for those areas before entering. When in doubt, cut power to the whole floor.

If you're unsure whether an area is safe, don't enter it — call a professional.

---

## Step 3: Document Everything Before You Touch It

Before you start moving furniture or soaking up water, grab your phone and document everything. Photos and video of the damage are essential for your insurance claim. Capture:

- The origin point of the burst (if visible)
- Standing water on floors, walls, and ceilings
- Damaged furniture, flooring, and belongings

Do this first. It takes five minutes and can save you thousands of dollars when dealing with your insurer.

---

## Step 4: Remove Standing Water — But Don't Overdo It

If you have a wet/dry shop vac, use it to pull up standing water on hard floors. Move rugs, furniture, and anything absorbent out of the wet area. Open windows if weather permits to start airflow.

What you should *not* do: try to dry out walls, subfloors, or insulation on your own. Water migrates fast and hides deep. What looks dry on the surface is often soaked underneath — and that's where mold starts.

## Step 6: Contact Your Insurance Company

Most homeowner's insurance policies cover sudden and accidental water damage from a burst pipe — but the key word is *sudden*. Slow leaks or neglected maintenance may not be covered.

Call your insurer as soon as the immediate emergency is handled. Let them know you've already contacted a restoration company and have documentation ready. A reputable restoration crew can also work directly with adjusters to streamline the claim process.

## What Makes Wichita Homes Especially Vulnerable

Kansas winters can be brutal. When temperatures drop below freezing — which happens regularly in the Wichita area — pipes in exterior walls, unheated crawl spaces, and garages are at serious risk of freezing and bursting. This is especially true in older homes with less insulation.

If you've had any crawl space moisture issues, consider [vapor barrier installation](/service/vapor-barrier-installation/) as a preventive measure. A dry crawl space is far less likely to cause freeze-related pipe problems, and it protects your foundation year-round.
