## What Is Soda Blasting — and Why Does It Work on Aluminum?

If you've got oxidized aluminum, corroded boat parts, a grimed-up trailer, or an older vehicle with surface buildup, soda blasting might be exactly what you need. It's one of the most effective and safest methods for cleaning aluminum — and it's become a go-to service for Wichita homeowners and business owners who want results without damaging what they're cleaning.

Soda blasting uses sodium bicarbonate (the same stuff as baking soda) propelled at high velocity through a specialized blasting machine. The abrasive force strips away oxidation, paint, grease, and grime — but because baking soda is relatively soft, it won't warp, pit, or gouge aluminum the way sandblasting can.

That's the critical difference. Aluminum is a soft metal. Traditional sandblasting can leave micro-pits and heat distortion. Soda blasting cleans aggressively without going too far — which is exactly what you want on surfaces you plan to repaint, coat, or just restore to their original look.

## How the Process Works

Here's what to expect when you bring in a soda blasting job:

**1. Assessment**
We look at the surface, the existing coating or buildup, and what the final goal is — bare metal, prep for paint, or just a clean surface.

**2. Prep and Masking**
We mask off areas that shouldn't be blasted (glass, rubber seals, chrome trim) and set up containment to manage the baking soda residue.

**3. Blasting**
The sodium bicarbonate is propelled through a pressure system at controlled PSI. The operator works in passes to strip the surface evenly without over-blasting.

**4. Rinse and Inspect**
Baking soda is water-soluble, so cleanup is straightforward. We rinse the surface, inspect for any remaining buildup, and do touch passes if needed.

**5. Post-Blast Recommendation**
Freshly blasted aluminum oxidizes quickly. We'll let you know if a primer, clear coat, or protective sealant should go on promptly to protect the work.
