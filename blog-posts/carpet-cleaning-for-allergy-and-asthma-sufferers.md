## Your Carpet Might Be Making You Sick

If you're constantly dealing with sneezing, itchy eyes, or waking up congested — and you live in the Wichita area — your carpet could be a major part of the problem. Kansas homes deal with a unique mix of allergens: ragweed, dust mites, pet dander, and mold spores from humidity swings. All of it settles into carpet fibers where your vacuum can't fully reach.

The good news? The right carpet cleaning approach can make a real, noticeable difference. The bad news? Not all cleaning methods are created equal — and some can actually make things worse before they get better.

Here's what actually helps.

---

## What's Hiding in Your Carpet

Carpet acts like a filter. It traps airborne particles and keeps them out of the air you breathe — until it gets full. At that point, foot traffic and air movement stir everything back up. Common culprits include:

- **Dust mites** – Microscopic creatures that thrive in warm, humid environments and are one of the leading triggers for asthma
- **Pet dander** – Even homes without current pets can carry dander from previous occupants for months
- **Pollen** – Tracked in from outside, especially brutal during Kansas ragweed season
- **Mold spores** – Especially in basements or areas with any moisture history
- **Cockroach debris** – A surprisingly common asthma trigger in older homes

Regular vacuuming helps, but it only removes surface-level buildup. Deep cleaning is what gets to the rest.

---

## What Actually Helps: Hot Water Extraction

The method that consistently performs best for allergy and asthma sufferers is **hot water extraction** — also called steam cleaning. Here's why it works:

1. **Heat kills dust mites** — Water heated above 130°F neutralizes the proteins that trigger allergic reactions
2. **Deep fiber penetration** — The cleaning solution and hot water reach deep into the carpet pile where allergens are trapped
3. **High-powered extraction** — The debris, moisture, and loosened contaminants are pulled out of the carpet, not just pushed around

## What Doesn't Help (Or Can Make It Worse)

Not all cleaning options are equal when allergies are a concern:

- **Dry cleaning / low-moisture methods** — These don't generate enough heat to kill mites or reach deep allergen deposits
- **Over-wetting** — If a carpet isn't dried properly, you create conditions for mold growth, which trades one problem for a worse one
- **Harsh chemical residues** — Some cleaning agents leave residue that can irritate airways, especially for kids with asthma

When you're booking a cleaning, ask specifically about the extraction method and drying time. A company that can't answer those questions clearly isn't a great fit for allergy-sensitive households.

---

## How Often Should You Clean?

For households with allergy or asthma sufferers, the general recommendation is:

- **Every 6 months** if you have pets or children
- **Once a year minimum** for standard households
- **After any water damage or flooding** — mold spores can establish in carpet within 24–48 hours

## Don't Forget the Air Ducts

Here's something most people miss: if your HVAC system is pushing allergen-laden air through dirty ducts, even a freshly cleaned carpet will get recontaminated fast. [Air duct cleaning](/service/air-duct-cleaning/) is a natural complement to carpet cleaning for anyone serious about reducing indoor allergens — and the two together make a much bigger impact than either one alone.
