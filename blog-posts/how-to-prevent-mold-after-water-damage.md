## The Clock Starts the Moment Water Hits Your Floor

If you've had a burst pipe, appliance leak, or storm flooding in your Wichita home, there's one thing you need to understand right away: **mold can begin growing within 24 to 48 hours** of water exposure. That's not a scare tactic — it's just how mold works.

Kansas weather doesn't help. Wichita summers bring heat and humidity that mold absolutely loves. Once it takes hold in wet drywall, carpet padding, or wood framing, you're no longer dealing with water damage alone. You're dealing with a mold problem on top of it.

The good news? Mold is largely preventable if you move quickly and do the right things first. Here's exactly what to do.

---

## Step 1: Stop the Source of Water First

This sounds obvious, but it's the step people sometimes skip in a panic. Before anything else:

- Shut off the water supply valve if it's a plumbing leak
- Contact your insurance company to document the damage
- Take photos and video before moving anything

You can't dry what's still getting wet.

---

## Step 2: Remove Standing Water Immediately

Every minute water sits, it soaks deeper into flooring, subfloor, and wall cavities. Use whatever you have — wet/dry vacs, mops, towels — to pull up surface water as fast as possible.

If the volume is significant (a basement flood, burst pipe, or storm intrusion), this is the point where a professional [water damage restoration](/service/water-damage-restoration/) team makes a real difference. Industrial extractors pull thousands of gallons that a shop vac simply can't match.

---

## Step 3: Dry Everything — Fast and Thoroughly

This is where most DIY attempts fall short. Getting the floor *surface* dry isn't enough. Moisture hides inside:

- Drywall cavities
- Carpet padding and the subfloor beneath it
- Wood framing and structural members
- Insulation

**What actually works:**
- Open windows and doors if outdoor humidity is low (check the forecast — don't ventilate on a humid Kansas evening)
- Run multiple fans pointed at wet areas
- Use a dehumidifier continuously and empty it frequently
- Remove wet carpet padding — it almost never dries fully and becomes a mold factory

The target is getting structural moisture readings below 16% for wood and below 1% for concrete. Without a moisture meter, you're guessing.

---

## Step 4: Pull Damaged Materials That Can't Be Saved

Wet drywall, soaked insulation, and saturated carpet padding are not worth trying to dry and keep. They hold moisture too well and too long. Removing them:

- Dramatically speeds up drying time
- Eliminates prime mold growth surfaces
- Lets you inspect the structure behind the walls

Cut drywall 12–18 inches above the waterline to expose wall cavities. Yes, it's more work to repair — but it's far less work than [mold remediation](/service/mold-removal/) down the road.

---

## Step 5: Disinfect All Affected Surfaces

Once surfaces are physically dry, treat them with an EPA-registered antimicrobial or a diluted bleach solution (1 cup bleach per gallon of water on non-porous surfaces only). This kills mold spores before they can colonize.

Don't skip this step just because things *look* clean and dry.

---

## Step 6: Monitor for the Next 7–10 Days

Mold doesn't always show up where you expect it. Check:

- Behind baseboards and under flooring edges
- Inside closets adjacent to the affected area
- Along ceiling lines if the water came from above
- Any musty odor — that's mold off-gassing before it's visible

If you smell it before you see it, don't wait.

---
